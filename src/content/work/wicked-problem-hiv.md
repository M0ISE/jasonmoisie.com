Some problems are complicated. Others are *wicked* — they resist definition, every attempt to solve them changes the problem, and there is no point at which you can say you're finished.

This essay uses bridges to get at the difference. Building a bridge is hard, but we know the shape of the answer: a person needs to cross a river. That single assumption makes the problem exponentially simpler, because anything that doesn't get a person across falls out of the solution space. But it also hides the better question — *why does anyone need to cross this river at all?* What political, social or economic pressure put them on the wrong bank?

PrEP is a drug that, taken daily, reduces the chance of contracting HIV by around 99%. On paper the answer is obvious: maximise uptake. In practice affordability, education, access and adherence all get in the way, and the moralism left over from the AIDS epidemic still shapes what politicians are willing to touch. In 2021, 27% of Australian HIV notifications came from heterosexual sex — up three points on the year before — while the communities driving that increase largely believed the virus had nothing to do with them.
